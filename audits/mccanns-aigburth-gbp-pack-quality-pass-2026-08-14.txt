THE DEFECT - ONE FOUND AND FIXED, IN THE CHECKER, NOT THE PACK
Every hours rule in check-gbp-packs.js reads ONE region: the "- Hours:" bullet
in the profile basics. hoursLine is cut at the first following bullet or blank
line and the time rule, the day rule and the day-pairing rule all read that
string and nothing else. This pack states its hours in FOUR places.

Proved by injection, all 36 checkers exiting 0 on each:

  a) paster note, Saturday close 5:00pm changed to 6:00pm   ALL 36 EXIT 0
  b) photo shot list, lunch closure end 2:00pm to 3:00pm    ALL 36 EXIT 0
  c) paster note, "Monday to Friday" to "Monday to Saturday" ALL 36 EXIT 0

(a) is the operative line. It is the sentence the paster reads while typing
into Google's hours editor, and it published a Saturday an hour longer than
the staff work while the guarded line stayed perfect. Same shape as the
address defect the 2026-08-13 pass closed on this same pack: a fact stated
more than once, guarded in one place only, and the unguarded copy the one
that reaches a human.

THE FIX
New rule in tools/check-gbp-packs.js: an hours statement anywhere in the pack
must agree with branches.json. Scope is the whole pack minus the already
guarded hours line. Composed from branches.json, nothing hardcoded.
- Quoted spans are not read, because clear-aintree.md and smartts-bootle.md
  quote the hours their own WEBSITE publishes as evidence the site is wrong.
- History parentheticals are not read (scorah-hazel-grove's ceased Saturday).
- Days branches.json holds CLOSED are skipped, which is what keeps scorah-
  hazel-grove's "if the profile still shows Saturday 9:00am to 1:00pm, remove
  it" out of scope: an instruction to delete a wrong day is not a claim.
- A lunch statement is read as a BREAK and checked against the gap the
  specification actually leaves, not as opening hours, so gordon-short-crosby's
  correct "closes for lunch 1:00pm to 2:00pm Monday to Saturday" passes.

A FAULT IN THE FIRST DRAFT OF THE RULE, FOUND BY ITS OWN NEGATIVE TESTS
The segment splitter treated the colon as punctuation, which it is in the
paster note ("... to show the lunch closure: Monday to Friday ..."), but a
clock time carries one too, so "9:00am" was cut into "9" and "00am" and the
rule read nothing. It caught injection (b), whose colons sat inside brackets
and so were never split, and MISSED (a) and (c). A colon between two digits is
now treated as part of a time. Recorded because a rule that passes its happy
path while silently reading nothing is the exact failure this pass exists to
find.
