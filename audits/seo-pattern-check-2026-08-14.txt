THE DEFECT: EVERY TOWN RULE ASKED THE PATTERN, NOT THE DATA
Item 3.1 reads "define the title/H1 pattern once, in the generator, with
per-branch town words SOURCED FROM branches.json". Build Pack v2 section 5.1
gives the reason: "Build every URL, title and H1 from seoTown/townSlug. Using
addressLocality targets the wrong catchment."

Nothing in the repo asserted that clause. Every town rule that existed before
this pass - checkTitle, checkH1, checkMeta, the exact match in
check-seo-pattern.js and the cross-town absence rule - takes the branch town
from seo-pattern.js pick(). pick() is also what the composers use. So the
question "does the page carry the right town" was being answered by comparing
the page with pick()'s own answer, which is circular: a pick() that started
reading addressLocality would move the pages and the expectations together.

WHY IT MATTERS HERE RATHER THAN IN PRINCIPLE
Eight of the fifteen live branches have seoTown != addressLocality, and the
difference is the entire purpose of the field:
  mccanns_aigburth        Aigburth      vs Liverpool
  mccanns_sandringham     St Michael's  vs Liverpool
  clearchemist_aintree    Aintree       vs Liverpool
  colemanleigh_liverpool  Walton        vs Liverpool
  gordonshorts_crosby     Crosby        vs Liverpool
  cherrylane_liverpool    Walton        vs Liverpool
  tiffenbergs_longmoor    Aintree       vs Liverpool
  riddings_timperley      Timperley     vs Timperley, Altrincham
Under addressLocality those eight would all target Liverpool. Cherry Lane and
Coleman and Leighs would compete with each other for one city word instead of
owning Walton, and Clear Chemist and Tiffenbergs the same for Aintree. That is
precisely the failure Build Pack v2 section 1.4 and 5.1 are written against.

PROVED BY INJECTION, TWICE, BECAUSE THE TWO HALVES FAIL DIFFERENTLY
  A. Drift injected into landingTitle/landingH1 only.
     CAUGHT, loudly and by name, by the self-test in tools/seo-pattern.js:
     "landing FAIL title missing seoTown 'Aigburth'" and the same for the H1,
     seven branches over. checkTitle still asked the honest pick() for the
     town, so the anchor held. This half was already safe.
  B. Drift injected into pick() ITSELF.
     DEFEATED that anchor: the self-test PASSED. The suite went red only by
     collateral - five of the seven page types are built from store objects
     whose town the generator resolves from b.seoTown before the pattern is
     ever called, so those pages did not move while the expectations did, and
     the mismatch was reported as 437 page failures with no indication of the
     real cause. Those generators are an accidental anchor, not a rule.
     The branch landing family has no anchor at all: build-branch-landing-
     pages.js hands the raw branch straight to landingTitle(), so page and
     expectation moved together and every rule above passed on those pages.
     Both McCanns landing pages silently became "Pharmacy in Liverpool", with
     identical titles and identical H1s on one shared domain.

THE FIX
tools/check-seo-pattern.js now carries a DATA-SOURCE RULE that asserts the
source directly against branches.json instead of against the pattern's own
answer: for every buildable branch, pat.pick(b).town must equal b.seoTown and
pat.pick(b).brand must equal b.brandLabel. It is the only rule in the file
that does not route through pick() to decide what it expects.

It carries a vacuity guard, the same convention as the empty-PAGE_TYPES and
empty-service-words guards already in the file: if no live branch had an
addressLocality differing from its seoTown, the comparison would pass
whichever field pick() read, so that state FAILS rather than quietly reducing
the rule to a check of nothing.
