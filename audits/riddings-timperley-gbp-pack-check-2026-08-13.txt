THE DEFECT FOUND THIS PASS: SUPERLATIVE CLAIMS WERE NOT DETECTED AT ALL.
tools/claim-patterns.js is the single shared definition of the wording the
house rule on weight loss copy does not allow. It is applied to the 177
generated pages by check-weight-loss-copy.js and check-service-links.js, and
to the 15 GBP packs by check-gbp-packs.js. Its only comparative entry was
  [/most effective (weight loss|treatment)/i, "comparative efficacy claim"]
so it caught one phrasing of a comparative claim and no other. The plainest
ways of writing the same claim went through untouched.

Injected one at a time into Riddings Post C, which is weight loss copy bound
for a public Google Business Profile, and therefore an advertisement:

  "This is the best weight loss treatment available."   MISSED
  "The best treatment for weight loss."                 MISSED
  "The UK's number one weight loss clinic."             MISSED
  "The leading weight loss clinic in Trafford."         MISSED
  "The fastest treatment we offer."                     MISSED
  "The safest weight loss treatment available."         MISSED
  "Our best-in-class weight loss service."              MISSED

Every one of those left all 31 checkers green. For contrast, "The fastest way
to lose weight" WAS caught, by the literal string "fastest way" in
EFFICACY_FAIL, which shows how narrow the existing cover was: the claim was
caught only when it happened to use one exact form of words.

WHY THIS IS THE PROHIBITED CLASS AND NOT A JUDGEMENT CALL. The house
reference, AI\RBH_WeightLoss_Advertising_Standards.md, names it in its own
words at line 26: information about medicines must be balanced and factual,
"X is used to treat..." rather than "X, the best/fastest/strongest treatment
for...". The list held no rule for best, fastest or strongest.

THE FIX, AT THE SHARED SOURCE. Three noun-anchored patterns added to
tools/claim-patterns.js, so packs and generated pages gain the cover together
and there is still exactly one definition:
  1. an article or possessive, then a superlative
     (best, finest, leading, top, safest, strongest, fastest, quickest,
     number one, no. 1), then within 40 characters and without crossing a
     sentence or line end, one of treatment, clinic, service, programme,
     program, plan, injection, jab or weight loss
  2. best, strongest, safest, fastest or quickest directly qualifying
     weight loss or slimming
  3. best-in-class

NOUN-ANCHORED ON PURPOSE. A bare superlative would have failed 11 packs on
ordinary English: 11 of the 15 branch packs say "otherwise the best
straight-on frontage shot" in their photo shot list, which is a direction to
a photographer and not a claim about a medicine, and those same 11 are the
only packs that use the word at all. The superlative only fails where it
qualifies the thing being sold.

TWO GAPS FOUND AND NOT FIXED, RAISED AS QUESTIONS
Q67. The business description ends "Open 9am to 6pm Monday to Friday." That
sentence was changed to "9am to 5pm" and nothing failed. Every opening-hours
rule in check-gbp-packs.js reads the "- Hours:" line only, so opening hours
stated in description prose are unchecked against branches.json. The
description is a published Google profile field, so this would publish a
wrong closing time with every checker green.
Q68. The catchment reads "Timperley, Altrincham and Trafford" in three
places. Trafford was changed to Stockport in all three and nothing failed.
The catchment rule checks only that the list LEADS with the branch's own
seoTown; membership beyond the lead is never compared with serviceAreaList.
Stockport is not a sister branch's town anywhere in branches.json, so the
sister-town rule was right to stay silent. This is an uncovered case, not a
rule bug.
