Treatment options your prescriber may discuss

This is an overview, not a recommendation. You cannot choose a medicine in advance. At your consultation the prescriber decides with you whether any treatment is suitable, and a medicine that is not suitable for you will not be prescribed. Every option sits alongside diet and activity changes.

Diet, activity and support

Every plan

Advice on eating, activity and habits, with follow-up reviews. For some people this is the right place to start without any medicine.

Your GP can also tell you about NHS weight management services.

Orlistat

Capsules, taken with meals

Orlistat is used alongside a reduced-calorie diet in adults with a BMI of 30 or more, or 28 or more with weight-related health risks. It works in the gut, reducing how much of the fat in food is absorbed.

Common side effects include oily or loose stools and wind, particularly after fatty meals. A lower-strength version is available from the pharmacist without a prescription.

Mounjaro (tirzepatide)

Weekly injection

Mounjaro is used for weight management alongside a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity in adults with a BMI of 30 or more, or 27 to under 30 with at least one weight-related health condition. It acts on two hormone receptors (GIP and GLP-1) involved in appetite.

The dose starts low and is increased over several weeks. Common side effects include nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation and indigestion. It can make the contraceptive pill less effective, and your prescriber will advise you on this.

Wegovy (semaglutide)

Weekly injection

Wegovy is used for weight management alongside a reduced-calorie diet and more physical activity in adults with a BMI of 30 or more, or 27 to under 30 with at least one weight-related health condition. It acts like a natural hormone (GLP-1) involved in appetite.

The dose starts low and is increased over several weeks. Common side effects include nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation and stomach pain.

Side effects listed here are not a full list. Your prescriber will go through the patient information leaflet with you before you start any medicine.

Is this service right for you?

A prescriber will assess your suitability at consultation. Nothing on this page is a guarantee of treatment, a specific medicine, or a specific outcome. Individual results vary.

Who this service may suit

  • Adults aged 18 and over
  • Typically a BMI in the overweight or obese range for your height (your prescriber will confirm this at assessment, and may also consider your ethnicity and weight-related health conditions)
  • Ready to combine any treatment with diet and lifestyle changes, not use it as a stand-alone fix

When this service is not right for you

  • If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, or trying to become pregnant, this service is not suitable, please speak to your GP or midwife
  • Under 18s are not seen under this service
  • Certain conditions (including a personal or family history of specific thyroid cancers, pancreatitis, severe gastrointestinal disease, or an active eating disorder) may mean treatment is not appropriate, the prescriber will screen for these and advise accordingly
  • If you are already taking other weight-loss or diabetes medication, tell the prescriber so they can check it is safe to proceed
  • If you feel unwell, have symptoms that concern you, or need urgent help, contact your GP or NHS 111, or call 999 in an emergency

Questions people usually ask

Is this an NHS service?
No. This is a private service. Consultations and any medication supplied are paid for privately.
What does it cost?
The consultation is from £39.99. If medication is prescribed, its cost depends on the medicine and dose, and it is confirmed before you agree to anything, so there are no hidden charges.
Will I definitely be prescribed medication?
No. The prescriber only prescribes where it is clinically appropriate for you. If it is not, they will explain why and discuss other options.
Can I ask for a particular medicine?
You can tell the prescriber what you have read about, and they will discuss it with you, but the decision is a clinical one made with you at your consultation. A medicine that is not suitable for you will not be prescribed.
How quickly will I receive my medication?
If prescribed in store, it is usually ready the same day once approved. Posted orders are usually dispatched within one working day.
Can you show me how to inject?
Yes. If an injection is prescribed, our pharmacists can show you in person, and we can support you each week if you prefer.
Do I need a GP referral?
No. You can book directly with Smartts Chemist. This service does not replace your GP, and the prescriber will refer you back to your GP if that is the more appropriate route.
