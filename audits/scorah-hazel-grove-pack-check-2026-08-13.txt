PART 2 - THE REAL FINDING, IN THE CHECKER RATHER THAN THE PACK.
Two efficacy rules existed, one per surface, and they did not agree. The
generated pages carry the shared 9-pattern list in tools/claim-patterns.js, via
check-weight-loss-copy.js rule 9 and check-service-links.js. The GBP packs
carried only the local EFFICACY_FAIL list inside check-gbp-packs.js, which is
not a superset of it.

Proved by injection into this pack, five phrases, one at a time, restoring
between each. Before the fix, check-gbp-packs.js returned exit 0 on all five
while tools/claim-patterns.js findClaim() failed all five:

  phrase                                        pack     page
  "fast weight loss"                            GREEN    FAILS (efficacy claim)
  "delivers results"                            GREEN    FAILS (promises results)
  "real results"                                GREEN    FAILS (promises results)
  "most effective weight loss"                  GREEN    FAILS (comparative)
  "that actually works"                         GREEN    FAILS (efficacy claim)

EFFICACY_FAIL has "rapid weight loss" but not "fast", and "proven results" and
"best results" but not "delivers results" or "real results".

Why it matters in this direction. The house standard splits weight loss copy in
two. A generated inner page is Regime 2, the page a customer chose to click
into. A GBP post is Regime 1, an advertisement pushed onto a public Google
profile, which is the stricter half. The looser rule was governing the more
exposed surface, and Post C of every one of the 15 packs is weight loss
advertising copy pasted verbatim into that profile.

THE FIX. check-gbp-packs.js now also requires tools/claim-patterns.js and
applies CLAIM_PATTERNS to every pack, alongside EFFICACY_FAIL. Both lists are
kept and both run, because neither contains the other: EFFICACY_FAIL holds
pack-specific wording the shared list has no reason to carry ("miracle",
"cure", "no side effects", "before and after", "risk free"). Neither list was
retyped into the other file, which is the fault claim-patterns.js exists to
stop. A new findClaims() helper mirrors findTerms() for regex patterns and
reports line number, matched wording and the shared list's plain-English
reason.

The rule was extended to TEMPLATE.md, which the pack loop excludes by name and
which is the one file in gbp-packs/ that propagates into future packs. That
needed a carve-out: the template teaches these rules by quoting the wording it
bans, so a flat scan failed the file whose job is to state the rule, three
times on one line. The cut is structural, from the "Rules for every pack"
heading to the first "## " heading, with lines blanked rather than removed so
reported line numbers still match the file. This is the same convention the
checker already applies to opening hours, where a quoted or "previously/ceased"
time is read as evidence rather than as a claim. A guard on the boundary itself
fails if that heading is renamed, so the carve-out cannot silently widen.
