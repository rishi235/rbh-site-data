FINDING (real, in-repo)
------------------------
Rule 6's SHORT_SCAN_DIRS covered only the three generated-page folders
(modules/service/pages, modules/switch/pages, modules/branch/pages) and
never gbp-packs, unlike rules 2 and 4 (VARIANT, MISSPELT) on the same
checker, which do scan gbp-packs via SCAN_DIRS. The checker's own file
header lists "the GBP packs" as one of the hand-written-copy venues it
exists to protect (gap #1 in the header comment), so this was a real,
unstated gap, not a documented exclusion.

Measured before fixing (the checker's own established convention): widening
SHORT_SCAN_DIRS to include gbp-packs naively (whole-file scan, matching how
generated pages are scanned) produced 46 failures across 15 packs. Every
one was a legitimate cross-branch reference in paster prose ("our sister
branch in Hazel Grove", "The mccannspharmacy.co.uk domain is shared with
Sandringham") or, after adding the same quote-masking rules 2/4 already
use, a bare street name in narrative prose ("home on Cherry Lane", "on
Riddings Road" - the same brand/street ambiguity rule 6's own header
comment already names for Cherry Lane and Riddings, just not yet extended
to bare street names without a house number). None were real leaks.

The one part of a GBP pack that is actually pasted verbatim onto the public
Google profile is the business description (section 1) - the same field
check-gbp-packs.js's own descriptionOf() extracts and holds to the
750-character limit. Scoping rule 6's new gbp-packs coverage to just that
field, and extending the existing street-address mask to also cover the
bare street name (stripped of its leading house number, e.g. "202 Cherry
Lane" -> also mask "Cherry Lane"), brought the false-positive count to
exactly one real, live finding (see below) and zero false positives across
all 16 packs (TEMPLATE.md excluded by name, the same way check-jsonld.js
and check-whatsapp-route.js exclude DRAFT-* - a template has no branch to
resolve against; its own description uses several trading names as worked
examples on purpose).
