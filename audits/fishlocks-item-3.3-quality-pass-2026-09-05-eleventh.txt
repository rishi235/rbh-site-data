METHOD
------

1. RULE 4 (AMBIGUOUS). data-branch on the Ainsdale UTI page changed from
   "Fishlocks Chemist Ainsdale" to the bare brandLabel "Fishlocks Chemist".
   CAUGHT: "data-branch read \"Fishlocks Chemist\", which is also the name of
   this branch's sister shop on the same website. It cannot say which of the
   two this page is; branchName (\"Fishlocks Chemist Ainsdale\") can." Note
   rule 2 (OWNER) alone would NOT have caught this, since a bare brandLabel is
   an accepted OWNER value in general; rule 4 exists specifically because the
   brand is shared here, confirming the checker's own docstring claim that
   rule 4 is "the rule that matters" for exactly this branch pair.

2. RULE 10 (SERVICELINK). The "See all conditions covered by Pharmacy First"
   link on the Ainsdale UTI page repointed from
   pharmacy-first-fishlocks-ainsdale.html to
   pharmacy-first-fishlocks-eccleston.html (the sister branch's own Pharmacy
   First page, same host, so the link resolves). CAUGHT: "links to
   \"pharmacy-first-fishlocks-eccleston.html\", a service page belonging to
   fishlocks_eccleston (...), but the page belongs to fishlocks_ainsdale
   (...). Both branches are served from www.fishlockpharmacy.co.uk, so the
   link resolves and the patient is quietly booked into the wrong pharmacy's
   service."

3. RULE 11 (SISTERLABEL). The visible text of the sister-branch link on the
   Ainsdale landing page changed from "Fishlocks Chemist Eccleston" to the
   bare "Fishlocks Chemist" (href left untouched, still correctly pointing at
   pharmacy-fishlocks-eccleston.html). CAUGHT: "its link to
   \"pharmacy-fishlocks-eccleston.html\" reads \"Fishlocks Chemist\", but
   build-branch-landing-pages.js's own sisterNote() logic would render
   \"Fishlocks Chemist Eccleston\"... A patient reads the label, not the
   href, so a stale one still misdirects even though rule 9 confirms the
   link itself resolves correctly." Confirms rule 9 (href) and rule 11
   (label) are deliberately separate checks, not duplicates.

4. RULE 3 / 5 / 6 (SCHEMANAME / SITEUNIQUE / SPLIT), one injection catching
   three rules at once. JSON-LD "name" on the Eccleston UTI page changed from
   "Fishlocks Chemist Eccleston" to the sister's branchName "Fishlocks
   Chemist Ainsdale". CAUGHT three ways simultaneously:
     - rule 3 (SCHEMANAME): "JSON-LD name \"Fishlocks Chemist Ainsdale\" but
       the page belongs to fishlocks_eccleston (...) - that is
       fishlocks_ainsdale's name, so Google is told this address belongs to
       another pharmacy"
     - rule 6 (SPLIT): "fishlocks_eccleston: its pages declare 2 different
       values for JSON-LD name (...), so one branch presents itself as more
       than one business depending on which generator built the page"
     - rule 5 (SITEUNIQUE): "www.fishlockpharmacy.co.uk: branches
       fishlocks_ainsdale and fishlocks_eccleston both publish JSON-LD name
       \"Fishlocks Chemist Ainsdale\" on one website, at PR8 3HN and PR7 5SZ,
       so Google is handed two Pharmacy records with one name and two
       addresses and has to guess whether they are one business or two"
   This demonstrates the three rules are not redundant: SCHEMANAME catches
   the page against its own owning branch, SPLIT catches the owning branch's
   internal inconsistency across its own pages, and SITEUNIQUE catches the
   estate-wide host-level collision - all three fire from one single-field
   edit because they read the same fact from three different angles.
