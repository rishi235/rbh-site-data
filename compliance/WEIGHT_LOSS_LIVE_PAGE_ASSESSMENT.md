## Which regime each page sits in

The reference splits the position in two, and the split decides everything.

- Regime 1, advertising: near-total prohibition on any POM reference, named or
  not. Covers leaflets, press, posters, paid search and social, organic posts
  on our own social channels, influencer and affiliate content, and the landing
  page an ad points at.
- Regime 2, inner website pages: a limited exemption. A page about the
  condition, which the consumer chooses to access, may carry non-promotional
  information on specific medicines, provided it is a fair overview of the
  treatment options and it is clear the customer is being offered a medical
  consultation that may or may not lead to a prescription. The homepage and a
  proactively linked landing page sit outside the exemption.

So naming Mounjaro on an inner page is not by itself a breach. Q16 put the
position too flatly when it said advertising prescription-only medicines to the
public is not permitted, full stop. What decides these five pages is not that
the medicines are named. It is how they are presented, and how the page is
reached.

## Element by element, against the reference

Verdict key: BREACH means it fails a rule in the reference as the page stands.
CONDITIONAL means it fails only if the page is ad-linked or read as
promotional. CLEAR means the reference permits it on an inner page.

### 1. "Real Results with Mounjaro" (section heading). BREACH.

The reference is explicit on product claims on inner pages: information about
medicines must be balanced and factual, "X is used to treat..." rather than
"X, the best/fastest/strongest treatment for...". A heading built on results
is the promotional presentation the exemption excludes, and it is the trigger
the reference names for enforcement on an inner page: action follows on
complaint about overtly promotional presentation.

### 2. "Mounjaro (tirzepatide) is one of the most effective weight loss
treatments available." BREACH.

This is the superlative form the reference gives as the worked example of what
an inner page may not say. It is a comparative efficacy claim about a POM. It
is the single clearest breach on the page.

### 3. The slider: "How much weight could you lose? ... You could lose up to
26kg (22.5% of your body weight)". BREACH.

Rule 13.9 in the reference: ads must not claim people can lose precise amounts
of weight within a stated period. Rate of loss claims are acceptable on inner
pages ONLY if compliant with the rest of the guidance, and the rest of the
guidance is not met here, because the same page carries a superlative product
claim and a results heading. An interactive tool that returns a personalised
figure from the visitor's own weight is a promise of outcome, not a balanced
overview. The ruled breach cited in the reference (Skinny Revolution, 7 Oct
2020) is the same shape.

### 4. "Clinical studies show it can help you lose up to 22.5% of your body
weight over 72 weeks". CONDITIONAL, and BREACH as it currently stands.

Attributed trial data on an inner page can be factual information. Here it sits
under the "Real Results" heading, immediately after the superlative claim and
immediately before "Ready to start your transformation?", so it reads as
promotion rather than a fair overview. The sentence is not the problem. Its
setting is. Note the qualifying sentence that follows it ("Your actual results
will depend on...") is the right instinct and does not cure the heading or the
superlative above it.

### 5. "From £39.99 including your consultation." CONDITIONAL, and BREACH as
it currently stands.

The reference permits a factual list of prices for available treatments on
pages other than the homepage, provided the list carries no product claims and
does not actively encourage choosing on price, with no highlighted special
offers and no free offers. This is not a factual price list. It is a single
lead price in the hero block, above the fold, before any eligibility wording,
on a page that then makes a superlative claim about a named POM. "From" pricing
in that position encourages entry on price. The reference also records "POM
only special offers or discounted prices" and "treatments start from ..." as
ruled breaches in ads (Phlo, Juniper, Hexpress, 9 Jul 2025), which is the
wording pattern here.

### 6. "Explore treatments: Wegovy (semaglutide) / Mounjaro (tirzepatide) /
Orlistat", plus step 2 of How it works, "Choose a treatment". BREACH of the
consultation condition, not of the naming rule.

Naming all three, including the non-POM Orlistat, with dosage form, is close to
the fair overview of treatment options the exemption asks for, and on its own it
would be defensible. What fails is the framing. The exemption requires it to be
clear the customer is being offered a medical consultation which may or may not
lead to a prescription. "Explore treatments" as a chooser, and a numbered step
telling the visitor to choose a treatment, put the medicine selection in the
patient's hands and imply the prescription is the outcome. The reference also
bars "Buy [product]" style controls on POM sites; a treatment picker is the
same journey with softer words.

### 7. "Ready to start your transformation?" CONDITIONAL.

Not a POM claim and not a weight claim. It belongs with the 2025-26 social
responsibility rulings in the reference, which turn on exploiting body image.
On its own it is mild. It should go with the section it sits in.

### 8. "Access medicated weight loss treatment" (hero) and "Weekly injection"
(twice). CLEAR on an inner page.

"Weight loss injections" and "once a week weight loss treatment" are ruled POM
references in ADS (Regime 1). On an inner page under the exemption, dosing
form is factual product information and is permitted in a balanced overview.
Do not strip these on an inner page. Do not let them near an ad.

### 9. The page title and URL: "Weight Loss Clinic at Smartts Chemist |
Bootle, Liverpool", /weight-loss-clinic-bootle.html. CLEAR.

The reference asks that website addresses do not name specific POMs in the core
URL. These do not, and neither does the SERP title. Nothing to fix.

### 10. Opening hours, Smartts. NOT A COMPLIANCE POINT, wrong, and wider than
Q16 recorded.

branches.json holds two sessions a day for Smartts, 09:00 to 13:00 and 14:00 to
18:00 Monday to Friday, sourced from the NHS pharmacy profile and confirmed
2026-06-24. The live weight loss page states "Mon-Fri 9am-6pm" with no lunch
closure. Q16 recorded that as a defect on that one page. Reading the site this

## The finding Q16 did not have: the homepage

Q16 was scoped to five inner pages. Reading the homepages this run found the
harder half of the exposure, because the homepage is the page the reference
treats as advertising and the page it says MHRA enforcement focuses on.

Every branch homepage read this run carries this line in its services list:

    Weight Loss Clinic: Innovative solutions that deliver results.
    Tried the rest? Now try the best.

Verified identical at Smartts, Riddings and SK Chemists. SK Chemists was chosen
as the control precisely because it is NOT one of the five: it has no old
weight loss page, so this is a shared homepage template and the line is
estate-wide, not a symptom of the old page. The other twelve homepages were not
read this run and should be assumed to carry it until checked.

Why it matters more than anything on the inner pages:

- It is a results claim and a superlative claim ("deliver results", "now try
  the best"), on the page the reference treats under Regime 1 rather than under
  the inner-page exemption. The exemption does not reach it.
- Its subject is a service whose outcome is a POM prescription, so the ASA
  reading in the reference (Chequp, 9 Jul 2025 and 17 Dec 2025) applies: an ad
  may offer a consultation, but must not indicate the likely outcome is a POM
  prescription, whether through its own content or through the page it links
  directly to.
- The homepage's own top navigation carries a prominent "BOOK NOW WEIGHT LOSS
  CLINIC" call to action. At the five branches, that route lands on a page
  headed "Real Results with Mounjaro". That is the Chequp pattern exactly: the
  consultation call to action and the POM landing page, joined. It also
  weakens the inner-page defence for the five, because a page reached by a
  headline call to action from the homepage is closer to a proactively linked
  landing page than to a page the consumer chose to access.

Note the same homepage list also carries "Blood Testing: Fast results" and
"Travel Vaccinations: Free assessment", which are service claims about
non-POM services and are a different and much smaller question.

## The one part inside this repo

The Smartts switch page carries a hand-written services grid in the CONFIG
block of tools/build-switch-pages.js. Its Weight Loss Clinic tile says "Support
that delivers results" and links to the old page above. Measured against the
reference, the wording is a results claim for a service whose outcome is a POM,
so it is a real issue and not only a house-style one. It is the only efficacy
claim in the 177 generated pages.

It was NOT changed this run. It is patient-facing regulatory copy, which the
autonomous window's own carve-out reserves for Rishi, and the wording change is
part of the decision Q16 asked for rather than a defect with one right answer.
The suggested replacement, taken from the compliance-swept Smartts GBP pack, is
"Pharmacist-led consultations and ongoing support". It stays in KNOWN_CLAIM in
tools/check-service-links.js until the decision lands.

## Verdict on Q16

The finding holds. Five live pages carry copy that fails the house reference,
and the reasons are firmer than Q16 stated, not weaker: the superlative claim
and the results heading fail the inner-page test outright, and the slider, the
lead price and the treatment picker fail it as the page is currently built.

Two corrections to how Q16 put it:

1. Naming Wegovy, Mounjaro and Orlistat is not itself the breach. The
   inner-page exemption allows named medicines in a balanced, factual overview.
   A fix that only strips the names would leave the superlative claim, the
   slider and the price behind and would still fail, while losing information a
   patient is entitled to see.
2. The exposure is wider than five inner pages. The homepage line is estate
   wide and sits in the stricter regime.
